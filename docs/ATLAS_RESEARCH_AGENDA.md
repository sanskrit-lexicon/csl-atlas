## 2. New testable hypotheses

Eight proposals, none a reword of the 25 IDs in
[`HYPOTHESIS_INDEX.md`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/docs/HYPOTHESIS_INDEX.md)

### PH1 · CANON-CORE — the citation canon is nested, not modular *(layer b)*

- **Claim.** The dict×text citation matrix has a *core–periphery (nested)* topology: each
  dictionary's cited texts are approximately a subset of the next-broader dictionary's,
  rather than falling into disjoint tradition communities.
- **Data.** [`ls_citation_edges.tsv`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/data/citations/ls_citation_edges.tsv)
  (1,701 edges, 11 dicts × 912 texts) +
  [`ls_citation_nodes.tsv`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/data/citations/ls_citation_nodes.tsv).
  **Ready now.**
- **Method.** NODF nestedness and bipartite (Barber) modularity on the binarised matrix,
  each against degree-preserving permutation nulls; permutation *p* per the Dror et al.
  protocol (§4).
- **Expected output.** One statistic pair (NODF vs Q) + a nested-order heatmap (V1).
- **Research read.** Decides A50's §4 framing quantitatively: "traditions" as additive
  strata of one canon vs genuinely disjoint canons — a WSC-CSDH-shaped network claim.
- **Learner read.** If nested, the top-of-core texts (Mahābhārata 56,818 cites / 8 dicts;
  Ṛgveda 38,187 / 7) are a *reading list every dictionary supports* — a "start here" panel
  for students.
- **Nearest existing.** A50's shared-canon claim (existence + ranking). **Delta:** tests the
  matrix *topology*, which A50 asserts only impressionistically.
- **Data-readiness:** ready now.

### PH2 · CITE-4AXIS — citation canon is a fourth independent axis of descent *(layer b)*

- **Claim.** Dictionary-pair citation-profile similarity (cosine over per-dict normalised
  citation vectors) is statistically separable from the content, convention, and
  microstructure axes on the documented L0 edges.
- **Data.** `data/citations/` +
  [`data/lexico/three_axis_comparison.json`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/data/lexico/three_axis_comparison.json).
  **Ready now** (9 of the 13 L0-edge dictionaries are among the 11 graph dicts; the
  shrunken edge set must be reported honestly).
- **Method.** Extend
  [`scripts/build-three-axis-independence.mjs`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/scripts/build-three-axis-independence.mjs)
  with a fourth column; same Pearson-vs-small-n-threshold protocol as THREE-AXIS-INDEP,
  plus a label-permutation robustness check.
- **Expected output.** A 4×4 correlation table + axis-pair scatters (V3).
- **Research read.** Either outcome is publishable: independence upgrades A03 to four
  components; collinearity with content would mean the citation apparatus travels with
  headword stock — sharpening APPARATUS-NOT-ERRORS.
- **Learner read.** "Two dictionaries that quote the same books" is the most intuitive
  kinship explanation the atlas can offer; the scatter doubles as a dictionary-chooser aid.
- **Nearest existing.** THREE-AXIS-INDEP (three axes); PET-MW-CITE (entry-level citation
  truncation on specific edges). **Delta:** graph-level canon profile as a *new axis*, not
  entry-level truncation evidence.
- **Data-readiness:** ready now.

### PH3 · FREQ-STRAT — dictionaries have era-frequency signatures *(layers a+c)*

- **Claim.** Each dictionary's headword stock has a distinctive DCS-period frequency
  signature, and family predicts the weighting: Vedic-track dictionaries (GRA) weight
  early-period lemmas, classical-track (AP/AP90) weight late/classical lemmas, and the
  Petersburg line sits between.
- **Data.** [`union_headwords.tsv`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/HeadwordLists/union/union_headwords.tsv)
  (per-dict provenance column) ×
  [`kosha/data/frequency/lemma_frequency.tsv`](https://github.com/gasyoun/kosha/blob/main/data/frequency/lemma_frequency.tsv)
  (83,277 rows; `periods` = per-DCS-period vector). **Join key: SLP1 lemma** (both files
  SLP1-keyed; fold via the vendored
  [`sanskrit-util`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/sanskrit-util) form key).
  **Needs derivation:** a per-dict period-profile table (`build-period-signatures.mjs`) —
  dictionary-facing, consumes the frozen public kosha release, no DCS ingestion
  (boundary-clean).
- **Method.** Per-dict normalised period-share vectors; family separation by
  Kruskal–Wallis with bootstrap CIs; χ² against the whole-union baseline.
- **Expected output.** Small-multiple period-share bars per dictionary (V4 panel).
- **Research read.** The first quantitative statement of *which Sanskrit* each dictionary
  records — the corpus-facing complement to OBS-R's who-copies-whom.
- **Learner read.** Evidence for "which dictionary for reading the Ṛgveda vs kāvya?" —
  feeds `/dictionary-chooser` with data instead of lore.
- **Nearest existing.** DCS-JOIN (Type 3, learner-difficulty routing); OBS-R (static
  redundancy). **Delta:** a *dictionary-evidence* claim about coverage stratification by
  era, consuming a frozen release rather than waiting on the VisualDCS contract extension.
- **Data-readiness:** needs the join derivation (S effort).

### PH4 · GHOST-STOCK — dictionary-unique headwords are disproportionately corpus-unattested *(layer c)*

- **Claim.** The 42.2% of union lemmas found in exactly one dictionary are
  corpus-unattested at a far higher rate than shared lemmas; unique-*and-attested*
  headwords concentrate in specialised lexica (BHS, IEG) — most "novelty" in general
  dictionaries is inherited-then-orphaned or artefactual, while specialised novelty is
  real.
- **Data.** `union_headwords.tsv` (`n_dicts`, `dicts`) ×
  [`data/dcs/dcs_lemma_summary.json`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/data/dcs/dcs_lemma_summary.json)
  (83,239 SLP1 lemmas, `attested` + `freqBand`, already consumed in-repo under the
  [VisualDCS contract](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/docs/VISUALDCS_CONSUMPTION_CONTRACT.md)).
  **Join key: normalised SLP1** (reuse the contract's `<k1>` normalisation). **Ready now.**
- **Method.** Attestation rate by `n_dicts` stratum with Wilson CIs; per-dict
  unique-attested share; logistic model `attested ~ n_dicts + family`.
- **Expected output.** Attestation-by-multiplicity curve + per-dict bars (V4 strip).
- **Research read.** Externally witnesses OBS-R's "novel stock" — separates recorded
  language from lexicographic sediment; a strong DH QA claim.
- **Learner read.** A per-headword "corpus-attested?" chip in Reader/Dossier lookup tells a
  student whether a word is reading vocabulary or dictionary-only.
- **Nearest existing.** OBS-R (containment census); H5 (130-row curated anomaly review).
  **Delta:** corpus attestation as an *external witness at full scale*, vs internal
  containment (OBS-R) and curated anomalies (H5).
- **Data-readiness:** ready now.

### PH5 · ORTHO-CLOCK — gloss-language orthography is a dating and descent signal *(layer d)*

- **Claim.** The density of pre-reform German spellings (*Thier→Tier*) in dictionary gloss
  text is (i) a monotone clock on publication date across PWG → PW → PWK → Schmidt, and
  (ii) elevated in descendants relative to date-matched independents — copied German
  glosses carry fossil orthography, making the *meta-language* a descent tracer.
- **Data.** [`ortho_drift/de_reform_map.tsv`](https://github.com/drdhaval2785/SanskritSpellCheck/blob/master/ortho_drift/de_reform_map.tsv)
  (15,685 pairs) +
  [`ru_reform_map.tsv`](https://github.com/drdhaval2785/SanskritSpellCheck/blob/master/ortho_drift/ru_reform_map.tsv)
  (7,709) × `../csl-orig/v02/{pwg,pw,pwk,sch,ccs}` gloss text. The French/English maps are
  too small for claims (18 / 76 rows — verified; the brief's 254/71 was stale) and are
  excluded. **Join key: surface token in gloss text.** **Needs derivation:** a per-dict
  drift census (`build-ortho-drift.mjs`); SanskritSpellCheck already ships per-dict
  `*_drift_report.txt` intermediates in
  [`ortho_drift/`](https://github.com/drdhaval2785/SanskritSpellCheck/tree/master/ortho_drift)
  — consume those first, derive only the gap.
- **Method.** Pre-reform token share per dict with bootstrap CIs; date regression;
  descendant-vs-independent contrast (permutation test); evaluation conventions per
  Bollmann (§4).
- **Expected output.** Drift-vs-year scatter + top-drifted-forms table (V5).
- **Research read.** A genuinely novel axis — lexicography as print culture; no existing
  atlas hypothesis touches the gloss language itself. LChange/ALP-shaped: "orthographic
  fossils in copied dictionary text."
- **Learner read.** Explains why searching PWG for modern German spellings fails, and
  powers a search-normalisation aid for 19th-century German/Russian glosses.
- **Nearest existing.** THREE-AXIS-INDEP's convention axis (Sanskrit-side markup).
  **Delta:** the *meta-language* (German/Russian gloss orthography), never measured in the
  atlas.
- **Data-readiness:** needs the census derivation (M effort).

### PH6 · HERITAGE-WIT — Heritage non-coverage triangulates the ghost stratum *(layer c)*

- **Claim.** MW entries *not* covered by the Heritage lexicon
  (`covered_flag = 0` in the crosswalk) are disproportionately corpus-unattested and
  low-multiplicity; the triple filter **MW-unique ∧ Heritage-uncovered ∧ DCS-unattested**
  isolates a high-precision ghost-candidate stratum, upgrading H5's manual QA queue into a
  ranked, evidence-graded worklist.
- **Data.** [`mw_heritage_crosswalk.tsv`](https://github.com/gasyoun/SanskritLexicography/blob/master/HeadwordLists/mw_heritage_crosswalk.tsv)
  (185,803 rows: `mw_key1 · covered_flag · heritage_entry_anchor`) × `union_headwords.tsv`
  × `dcs_lemma_summary.json`. **Join key: MW SLP1 key (`mw_key1`) ↔ normalised SLP1.**
  **Ready now.**
- **Method.** Coverage/attestation cross-tabulation with odds ratios + bootstrap CIs;
  precision spot-check of the triple-filter stratum against source lines
  (`/spot-check-sample` discipline, κ-reported adjudication per §4).
- **Expected output.** A 2×2×2 witness cube summary + a ranked ghost-candidate table
  routed to the H5 review queue (not asserted as fact — `inferred` until reviewed).
- **Research read.** Two independent modern witnesses (corpus + Heritage lexicon) joined
  at full MW scale — the external-validation layer OBS-R and H5 both lack.
- **Learner read.** A third dossier chip: "confirmed by the Heritage dictionary" — a
  modern-lexicon second opinion a student can actually check (license: cite/call Heritage,
  never clone — LGPLLR).
- **Nearest existing.** H5 (ghost anomalies, curated); OBS-R (uniqueness). **Delta:**
  external-witness triangulation at scale with a ranked queue as output, vs H5's 130
  hand-picked rows.
- **Data-readiness:** ready now.

### PH7 · CANON-DATE — later dictionaries cite a broader canon *(layers a+b)*

- **Claim.** Citation-canon breadth (distinct texts; Shannon entropy of the citation
  vector) increases with publication year, controlling for dictionary size and family —
  19th-century philology's accumulating text availability is visible inside the
  dictionaries' apparatus.
- **Data.** `data/citations/` ×
  [`data/dictionary_inventory.csv`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/data/dictionary_inventory.csv)
  (`year` / `start_year` / `end_year`). **Ready now** (11 dicts — small *n*: report effect
  sizes + CIs, not significance theatre).
- **Method.** Entropy and text-count vs year with the H1R discipline applied from the
  start: within-family deltas first, then a saturated OLS with honest bounds.
- **Expected output.** Breadth-vs-year dot plot with family lanes (V2 overlay).
- **Research read.** Ties layer (b) to layer (a); either result refines A50 §4
  (accumulation vs stable canon).
- **Learner read.** "Newer dictionary ⇒ more of the literature reachable from it" becomes
  a concrete, checkable reason to start with MW rather than WIL.
- **Nearest existing.** H1R (temporal claim about sense granularity — negative, and the
  source of the family-control lesson); A50 §4. **Delta:** canon *breadth* as the response
  variable — a different object measured with H1R's hard-won controls.
- **Data-readiness:** ready now.

### PH8 · HEAP-SAT — union headword growth saturates *(layers a+c)*

- **Claim.** Accumulating dictionaries in publication order, the union headword curve
  follows a saturating (Heaps-type) growth law: each new *general* dictionary after ~1890
  adds <5% novel lemmas, while specialised lexica (BHS, IEG, technical kośas) arrive as
  visible breaks above the fitted curve.
- **Data.** `union_headwords.tsv` (provenance) + `dictionary_inventory.csv` (dates).
  **Join key: dict siglum.** **Ready now.**
- **Method.** Heaps-law fit K·n^β on cumulative distinct lemmas; per-dict marginal-novelty
  residuals; order-permutation null for the specialised-break claim.
- **Expected output.** Cumulative growth curve with per-dict step annotations (V4).
- **Research read.** The dynamic complement to OBS-R: redundancy as the *equilibrium* of a
  saturating recording process — a clean, figure-first result for the measurement-framework
  paper (P1).
- **Learner read.** An evidence-based answer to "do I need more than MW + Apte?" — the
  plateau is the answer; the breaks say when a specialised dictionary genuinely adds.
- **Nearest existing.** OBS-R (static census). **Delta:** a growth *model* over
  publication time vs a snapshot; lexicographic saturation itself becomes the object.
- **Data-readiness:** ready now.

## 4. ACL-Anthology method crosswalk

All URLs fetch-verified 07-07-2026. Venue context: the Computational Sanskrit & Digital
Humanities track ([2023.wsc-csdh](https://aclanthology.org/volumes/2023.wsc-csdh/),
[2025.wsc-csdh](https://aclanthology.org/volumes/2025.wsc-csdh/)) and the Ancient Language
Processing workshop ([2025.alp-1](https://aclanthology.org/volumes/2025.alp-1/)) contain

material is an open niche at its most natural venue. (The correction lane's own ACL table

**What to adopt first.** (1) The **Dror et al. significance protocol** and
**Artstein–Poesio agreement reporting** are zero-derivation discipline — adopt as standing
requirements in
[`CHART_TRUST_TEMPLATE.md`](https://github.com/sanskrit-lexicon/csl-atlas/blob/main/docs/CHART_TRUST_TEMPLATE.md)
so every figure carries the right test and every adjudication a κ. (2) The **AAN
citation-network recipe** is the shortest path to making A50 real: the edges are committed;
what is missing is exactly what Radev et al. standardised. (3) **Phylogenetics validation**
(Rama et al.) is the one method that would materially harden the L0 stemma for reviewers.
(4) The **MWSA label protocol** is the right frame for sense work but stays *deferred*
until the R2 parser packet unblocks the cross-language pair — do not prototype early.
