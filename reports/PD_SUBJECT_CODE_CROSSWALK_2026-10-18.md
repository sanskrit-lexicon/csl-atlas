## What this is

H6930 classifies PD's citation apparatus into nine genre classes. This crosswalk
expresses those classes in PD's own terms: the **62 printed subject classes** of
the Subject-wise Classification. The same 1–62 numbers are the subject codes of
the printed **Index of Works** (pp. LXXXIX–CXXVII); H6945 transcribes that
per-work index separately. The `index_of_works_subject_codes` column is therefore
the join key H6945 can use without re-deriving the classification.

The mapping is one-to-many. It follows H6930's classifier rather than imposing a
new taxonomy: for example, H6930's *kavya* class absorbs Kāvyaprakāśa,
Nāṭyaśāstra and Daśarūpaka, so printed classes 48–51 (Poetics, Dramaturgy,
Nṛtya, Prosody) belong to *kavya*, not to *medieval*.

## Boundary calls

- **22 Siksas → vedic.** H6930 treats prātiśākhya, śikṣā and nirukta as Vedic
  auxiliary texts, not as grammar.
- **16 Jainism: Narrations → kavya; 15 Jainism: Dogmatics → medieval.** H6930
  classifies narrative Jain works as kavya and dogmatic works with the
  post-classical commentarial corpus.
- **24 Commentaries on the Amarakośa → lexicography.** The class is lexical, not
  grammatical, in H6930's usage.
- **48–51 → kavya.** H6930's classifier already places alamkāra and nāṭya works
  (Kāvyaprakāśa, Nāṭyaśāstra, Daśarūpaka) in kavya; prosody stays with that
  aesthetic cluster rather than being forced into medieval.

## Shares do not move

The genre table is still generated by the H6930 classifier. The crosswalk only
adds the `printed_subject_codes` column; all eleven pre-existing columns are
byte-identical to the H6930 table, including both denominators and every share.
