<h2 style="font-size:1.05rem;margin-top:1.5rem">Within-family controls — the sharper test</h2>
<p class="note">Year is a dict-level covariate with only <b>11 values across ~7 families</b>, so the pooled Pearson r conflates time with family composition. The honest test holds family fixed: the four families covered by ≥2 dicts each give a <b>within-family</b> trend (panel units, which also remove the MW splitting confound).</p>

<p class="note"><b>Every</b> family declines (mean −0.11 units/lemma·yr) — the opposite of inflation. A family-controlled OLS (<code>panelUnits ~ year + family</code>, n=11) gives year's partial slope <b>−0.051/yr, 95% CI [−0.186, +0.084]</b> — negative, but the CI spans zero because 11 dicts over 7 families leave only 3 residual df (<b>near-saturated</b>: the slope is bounded, not measured). Within the single western-parsed regime (n=9) the bivariate r is −0.11, so the family-granularity gap is lexicographic, not a parser artifact. New block: <code>h1Controlled</code> in <code>data/lexico/r2_h1_panel.json</code>; per-(lemma,dict) rows in <code>data/lexico/r2_h1_panel_lemma.json</code> (330 obs).</p>

<p class="note"><b>Trust Block.</b> Evidence: <code>data/lexico/r2_h1.json</code> (full-corpus per-dict means, 11 dicts; Pearson r = 0.036 vs archived 0.06) and <code>data/lexico/r2_h1_panel.json</code> (fixed 30-lemma panel; pooled Pearson r = 0.093 vs archived 0.01, plus the <code>h1Controlled</code> within-family block). The pooled r is <b>underidentified, not a measured null</b>; held family-fixed, granularity is flat-to-<b>decreasing</b> over time, so H1 (sense granularity inflates over time) is contradicted in direction, not merely unsupported. Limitations: each within-family slope rests on as few as 2 dicts; the OLS is near-saturated (CI bounds the year effect, does not estimate it); panel biased toward medium-to-high sense-count nouns; MW 1872 outlier (24.3) reflects sparse nominal coverage. Validation: <code>npm run build-r2-h1-panel</code> + <code>npm run build-r2-pages</code>; 187/188 tests pass (the one failure is the unrelated <code>sanskrit-util</code> sibling drift-guard). Owner repo: <code>csl-atlas</code>.</p>
<p><b>Finding.</b> Granularity clusters by FAMILY, not year: enumerating traditions (Apte, Benfey) pack many sense-units/entry; lumping traditions (Monier-Williams, Petersburg, indigenous) ~1. The pooled year-trend is near-zero (Pearson r = 0.036 full-corpus, 0.093 deconfounded panel), but that r is <b>underidentified</b> — year is pinned to 11 dict-points spread over ~7 families. Held family-fixed, the trend is not just absent but <b>negative</b>: all four multi-dict families decline over time (mean −0.11 panel-units/lemma·yr), and the family-controlled year slope is −0.051/yr (95% CI [−0.186, +0.084], near-saturated). So H1 (pure temporal inflation) is contradicted in direction; sense granularity is a tradition / marking-style trait, which Paper L must CONTROL FOR — and any future claim of inflation is falsifiable as a single family showing a clear per-year increase.
The variance is captured by family
(means: Benfey 2.53, Apte 2.35, Monier-Williams 2.06, Wilson 1.8, Cappeller 1.35, Petersburg 1.15, indigenous 1.09).</p>
