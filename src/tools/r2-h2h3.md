<h2>Robustness — the citation effect is edge-concentrated</h2>
<p class="note">The H2 chart above is the <b>unadjusted</b> gap, and it is fragile. Cited ancestor senses are <b>not spread across the panel</b>: Wilson 1832 carries almost no <code>&lt;ls&gt;</code> citations, so <b>82 of 84 cited senses sit on a single edge — Apte 1890 → 1957</b> (the other two edges have one cited sense each). That concentration makes the pooled controlled regression unreliable: refitting <code>survived ~ cited + centrality controls + edge fixed effects</code> (lemma-cluster-robust) gives a citation OR of 3.0 (p = 0.011), but the estimate <b>moves with unrelated parsing choices</b> — the YAT semicolon promotion (#126) alone shifted it from ≈1.75 to ≈3.0 without touching a single cited sense. The pooled OR is therefore <b>not</b> a trustworthy citation effect.</p>
<p class="note"><b>The clean test</b> is within the one citation-bearing edge, where parsing of the others is irrelevant. On Apte 1890 → 1957: cited senses survive at <b>0.768</b> (n = 82), uncited at <b>0.661</b> (n = 221) — a two-proportion <b>z = 1.80, p = 0.072</b>, <b>not significant</b>. So citation <i>co-varies</i> with survival but is <b>not established as an independent predictor</b>; a larger, multi-edge panel with citations on more than one edge is needed. (<code>h2Controlled.withinPrimaryEdge</code> + <code>citedByEdge</code> in <code>data/lexico/r2_h2h3.json</code>.)</p>
<p class="note"><b>Threshold sensitivity (referee item M4).</b> The pooled OR is significant at every Jaccard cutoff from 0.10 to 0.25 — but that stability just inherits the edge-concentration fragility above; it is <b>not</b> evidence for H2. (Note the naive gap itself collapsed to ~0.03–0.06 once YAT is counted properly, from ~0.17 before.)</p>
